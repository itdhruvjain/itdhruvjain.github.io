Before the demo · why this role matters

Hydrogen and oxygen don’t make water on their own.

You can have both and still have no water. Something has to bring them together. A team works the same way: your founder, operations, ads, SEO, IT, support and VAs each do good work, but the value comes from how it connects.

This role sits in the middle. The person in it has to work with almost everyone, understand them and their work, and build the tools that join it all up. So choose them carefully. I believe I’m the best fit for it, and the rest of this page is my case.

01 · Research

What I learned about your business

Everything here comes from public sources. Where I’m guessing, I say so.

How you sell

A Shopify store (with Shogun pages and Fast Bundle offers), Meta and YouTube ads, sponsored articles on lifestyle sites such as Esquire AU and HollywoodLife, and listings on German online pharmacies (medpex, DocMorris) with PZN codes. Products are about €54–69, led by the 10 ml Deep Wrinkle Filler Gel.

So what for engineering: one product list that maps each SKU to its PZN, and UTM tags on every link, so every channel’s sales are counted the same way.

Who buys

People with mature skin across Europe, the UK, the US, Canada and Australia, buying a premium product backed by a 60-day money-back guarantee. Most probably shop on their phones (assumption).

So what for engineering: notices and emails written plainly and designed for mobile first, and measured with the Clarity and heatmap tools already on the site.

How the team works

Small and fully remote, largely in the Philippines. You’re hiring for ops, ads, SEO, IT and VA roles at the same time, and a VA builds the Ads Manager reports by hand in spreadsheets.

So what for engineering: tools that are simple, documented and work across time zones, starting with whatever removes manual spreadsheet work.

What customers complain about

Mainly surprise import duties collected by FedEx at the door (for example US $55 on an $89 order, and UK £51 and £68). Also menthol irritation, a small tube for the price, results that take weeks, and limited local availability. The rating is 4.8, but negative reviews get no replies.

So what for engineering: most of these can be fixed by showing the right information at the right moment, before the customer has to ask.

03 · The biggest fix

The duties problem

  1. The customer pays €67 at checkout. The price looks fair, and the guarantee makes it feel safe.
  2. The courier asks for more at the door. Duty, tax and a handling fee, which can come to more than half the order.
  3. They leave a 1-star review. The product may be fine, but the surprise is what they remember.

Assumption If you ship duties-unpaid from the EU to the UK, US, Canada and Australia (which the reviews suggest), the courier collects these fees on delivery. The explainer below uses illustrative numbers.

Rates, thresholds and fees are round numbers from data/duties.js, chosen to show the shape of the problem. They are not current rules. Check the real thresholds for each market before using anything like this with customers.

Three options, from cheapest to best

  1. a
    Clear notices on the product, cart and post-purchase pages

    Ships in days and costs almost nothing. It takes away the surprise, but not the charge.

  2. b
    Show estimated duties at checkout

    The customer sees an honest total up front, but estimates can be off and they still pay the courier at the door.

  3. c
    Duties-paid shipping (DDP) through Shopify Markets / Managed Markets, Zonos or Global-e

    Nothing to pay at the door, which is the best experience. It costs a fee or some margin, and needs HS codes and carrier support.

05 · Plan

First 30 days

One shipped outcome a week, each with a number we can watch.

  1. Week 1Ads reporting automation

    The weekly ads report builds itself from Meta, YouTube and Shopify, and spend spikes alert the media buyer in Slack.

    Metric: VA hours saved per week

  2. Week 2Duties notices and post-purchase email

    Clear import-fee notices on the product, cart and confirmation pages, plus a pre-delivery email for non-EU orders.

    Metric: duty-related tickets and reviews

  3. Week 3Support triage with drafted replies

    Every email is tagged, matched to its order and checked against policy. A person approves each draft, and anything unclear goes straight to a person.

    Metric: first-response time

  4. Week 4Review replies, theme reports, stuck-parcel alerts

    Drafted replies to negative reviews, a weekly themes note for the product team, and alerts before customers ask where their parcel is.

    Metric: share of negative reviews answered; WISMO tickets

What I’d ask on day 1

  1. Which helpdesk or inbox does support use today?
  2. Which 3PLs and carriers ship each market, and do non-EU parcels go duties-unpaid?
  3. Which Shopify plan are you on, and are Shopify Markets or Managed Markets an option?
  4. Does your Trustpilot plan include the API module?
  5. Do all Meta and YouTube ads carry UTM tags, and where does the VA’s report live today?

06 · How I build

Engineering principles

  • Simple over clever. Small services and plain rules that the next person can read.
  • People approve anything customer-facing. Automations draft and suggest. They don’t send on their own.
  • GDPR-aware. No customer data in logs, hosted in an EU region, and only the data each task needs.
  • Documented. Every automation has a one-page “what, why and how to switch it off”.
  • Small, reversible releases. Behind a switch, rolled out one market at a time.
  • AI drafts are checked. Replies start from approved templates and real order facts, and a person reviews them before they go out.
